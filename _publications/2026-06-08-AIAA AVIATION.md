excerpt: "Approach and landing remain safety-critical phases of flight, with wind conditions further complicating aircraft control, energy management, and pilot decision-making during final approach. While stabilized approach criteria are widely implemented to enhance landing safety, their threshold-based classifications offer limited insight into wind-related variation in approach performance. This study introduces a data-driven framework to examine the relationship between reported wind exposure and approach stability by leveraging Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) trajectory data, surface weather observations, and runway reference geometry. The analysis focused on 2,064 Cessna 172S training approaches conducted by a Part 141 training fleet at Daytona Beach International Airport (DAB) over a three-month period. Multiple regression models were used to evaluate associations between three wind exposure variables and five stability indicators. Results indicated that wind exposure was more consistently associated with variability-based indicators than with path-deviation indicators, with the most pronounced relationship observed for vertical rate variability. These findings suggest that wind effects during approach are more clearly reflected in short-term control variability than in sustained path displacement, highlighting the limitations of threshold-based stability assessments in capturing instability patterns. The proposed framework offers a scalable method for evaluating approach stability using routinely available trajectory and weather data."
