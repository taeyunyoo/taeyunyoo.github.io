excerpt: "This study presents an empirical analysis using Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast trajectories to investigate relationships between runway-relative surface wind and final approach stability in collegiate flight training. The analysis drew on surveillance data covering one year of Cessna 172S approaches at Daytona Beach International Airport. Following trajectory processing and integration with time-matched surface weather observations, the final analytical sample comprised 5,054 approaches. Crosswind, longitudinal wind, and gust factor were assessed against lateral and vertical deviation root mean square errors and track, vertical rate, and groundspeed variability using five multiple linear regression models with heteroskedasticityconsistent standard errors and Holm-adjusted 𝒑-values. All five regression models were statistically significant, indicating that the examined wind exposure measures were jointly associated with each of the five approach stability outcomes. Crosswind and gust factor were positively associated with lateral deviation, track variability, and vertical rate variability, whereas longitudinal wind showed the strongest positive association with groundspeed variability. These findings distinguish path deviation from trajectory variability and support scalable assessment of wind-related variation in training operations."
